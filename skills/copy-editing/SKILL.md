# Copy Editing

## Goal

The draft reads as one continuous argument. No paragraph could be moved or deleted without the reader noticing.

## What to check, in order

1. **Spine.** State the piece's single claim in one sentence. If you cannot, the draft has no spine. Flag it before editing anything else.
2. **Order.** Does each section answer the question the previous one raised? Reorder where the reader would ask "why am I being told this now?"
3. **Paragraph logic.** One idea per paragraph. Split paragraphs carrying two, merge those carrying half.
4. **Transitions.** Cut mechanical connectors. A good order needs few of them.
5. **Repetition.** Remove ideas restated in different words. Keep the strongest version.
6. **Consistency.** Same term for the same thing throughout. Same tense, same person, same level of formality.
7. **Opening and closing.** Opening earns the next sentence. Closing lands the claim without recapping.

## Boundaries

- Preserve the author's voice and claims. Edit the carrying, not the cargo.
- Do not rewrite sentences for style unless the sentence blocks the flow. That is the previous stage's job.
- Do not fix spelling or punctuation here. That is the next stage.
- When something is cut, say what was cut and why in a short note after the text.

## Done when

- You can read the headings alone and follow the argument.
- No paragraph begins with a filler transition.
- The change note lists every structural move.
