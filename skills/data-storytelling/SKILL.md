# Data Storytelling

## Goal

The reader finishes knowing one thing they did not know before and what to do about it.

## Before drafting

- Ask what decision the reader has to make. The whole piece serves that decision.
- Identify the single most surprising or consequential finding. That is the spine. Everything else supports it or gets cut.
- Know the audience's numeracy. Executives want the conclusion first, analysts want the method visible.

## Boundaries

- Never present a number without its comparison: against last period, against the target, against the alternative. A lone figure means nothing.
- Never pad with statistics that do not change the conclusion.
- Never hide an inconvenient result. If the data cuts against the recommendation, say so and explain why the recommendation still holds, or change it.
- Distinguish correlation from cause explicitly.
- Charts only where a sentence cannot do the job. Describe what each chart must show, not how it looks.

## Done when

- The lead paragraph states the finding and the recommended action.
- Every number in the piece has a stated source and a comparison point.
- A reader who stops after the first screen still gets the decision.
