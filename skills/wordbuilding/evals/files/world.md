# Saltlands: World Notes

(Author note: "rough brain dump, some parts still blank")

## Geography

The Saltlands lie at the bottom of a dried inland sea. At the center stands Great Salt, a white salt dome formed in the deepest basin of the vanished sea. To the north rises the Brokenpeak range; to the south stretch flat salt plains. Rain falls on the northern slope of Brokenpeak, leaving the southern slope and salt plains arid. A few brine lakes remain from the old sea; there are no rivers between them, only a periodic flood called the "White Surge" every twelve years when Brokenpeak snowmelt sweeps across the flats. I haven't figured out where all the water goes or why it happens every twelve years yet.

Cities: Springtown sits on the northern slope where freshwater springs emerge. Saltkeep sits on a hill beside Great Salt above the reach of the White Surge. I didn't write why it was founded there—probably salt trade.

## Cool Elements

- Great Salt: hollow inside, housing a lost ancient city.
- Salt Singers: priests with white-sheened eyes who can melt salt with vocal resonance.
- The Seventh Carpet: a lost relic everyone believes will one day quiet the storms.
- Nine Founding Gods (haven't named them yet).
- Four eras—Salt Age, Prior Age, Sand Age, Deep Age—each marked by great wars.
- A second sleeping god beneath Brokenpeak.

## Peoples

The Salt Singers are a sect who revere the hollow city inside the salt dome. Ordinary folk worship Ironhearth and wear iron bracelets in his name. Northerners and southerners dislike each other: northerners are called "Wets" and southerners "Salts." Caravans haul salt along the road between towns. Some Salt Singers also worship Ironhearth while others reject him, causing constant internal disputes.

History: Four thousand years ago the War of Gods ended and the Salt Age began. In the Prior Age the inland sea dried up. Three hundred years ago Saltkeep suffered a major siege.

## Magic

Salt Singers can melt salt, which is how Saltkeep's walls were shaped. Singing salt into new forms is exhausting, limited to one hour per day. It isn't used in war because armies wear iron armor rather than salt.

I'm running a D&D campaign with friends set in the Saltlands. I want the world to feel deep, and I'm not sure if it does yet. I'm especially curious about Level 6 (infrastructure and institutions)—should I start building there?
