# Scientific Writing

## Goal

Produce a manuscript section or full paper that a reviewer in the field would accept as rigorous, clear, and honest.

## Before drafting, ask or infer

- Target venue and its author guidelines (word limits, abstract type, citation style).
- Study type, so the right reporting guideline applies: CONSORT for trials, STROBE for observational, PRISMA for reviews, ARRIVE for animal studies, CARE for case reports.
- Citation style. Do not assume one. If unknown, ask.
- Which sections are wanted. Do not write a whole paper when one section was requested.

## Boundaries

- Never invent a citation, a dataset, or a result. If a source is needed and not supplied, mark it as `[CITATION NEEDED]` and say so.
- Do not fabricate figures, graphical abstracts, or schematics. Suggest a figure only where the data genuinely needs one, and describe what it should show.
- Final text is full prose paragraphs. Bullet points are for the outline stage only.
- Past tense for methods and results, present tense for established facts and interpretation.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation. Do not overstate findings.
- Respect word limits strictly.

## Process

1. Outline each section with key points and the evidence behind each.
2. Convert the outline into paragraphs. Start with Methods, then Results, Discussion, Introduction, Abstract, Title.
3. Revise for one red thread from question to conclusion, consistent terminology, and guideline compliance.

## Done when

- Every claim traces to supplied data or a real cited source.
- The applicable reporting checklist is satisfied.
- No section exceeds the venue's limit.
- The abstract can stand alone and matches the body.
