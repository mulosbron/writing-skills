# Blog Writing Guide

## Goal

A post a knowledgeable reader would forward to a colleague. If it would not be shared, it needs more depth or belongs in a changelog.

## Voice

Sound like someone explaining a thing they actually understand to a peer. Direct, concrete, a little opinionated. Address the reader as "you". Use "I" or "we" honestly, whichever matches the author.

## Opening

The first two sentences state the problem or state the conclusion. Never background, never company history, never hype.

## Structure: answer the reader's questions in order

1. What problem does this solve?
2. How does it actually work? This is the bulk. Be specific.
3. What were the trade-offs or alternatives, and what was rejected and why?
4. What should the reader do next?

For deep-dives also cover what was tried and failed, and known limitations.

## Standards

- Headings carry information. "Why pre-aggregation destroys debugging context", not "Background".
- A performance or scale claim comes with its number.
- Code in a post has been run. Include imports and context.
- A system with more than two interacting parts gets a diagram, labelled with real names.
- Say "beta", "partial", "limited" when true. Never overstate.
- Title makes a specific claim or promises a specific payoff. Not "Introducing X".
- Close with something useful: a link, a next step, a question. No recap, no "can't wait to see what you build".

## Boundaries

- Do not write generic marketing copy. Specifics or nothing.
- Do not pad. The risk of being shallow is bigger than the risk of being long, but every paragraph must earn its place.
- Ask who the post is for before drafting if it is not obvious.

## When editing a draft

Quote the weak passage, say why it is weak, rewrite it to show the standard.
